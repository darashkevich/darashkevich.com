# Executive outbound playbook

You are not applying for jobs. You are selling an executive capability:

> I know how to build a world-class customer organization before it becomes a bottleneck.

Executive searches fill a large share of leadership roles through relationships, referrals, and direct outreach — not public postings. This playbook is the operating system for that pipeline.

## Positioning (what you sell)

Four things only:

1. **Scaling support organizations**
2. **AI-enabled customer operations**
3. **Subscription / eCommerce expertise**
4. **Executive operational leadership**

Not Zendesk. Not “customer support.” **Business scaling.**

When a founder opens your LinkedIn after a message, they should think: *This person already thinks like one of our executives.*

## Channel mix (target allocation)

| Channel | Share | Notes |
|---------|-------|--------|
| Proactive outreach to founders / executives | 40% | Primary engine |
| Warm introductions (investors, advisors, former colleagues) | 30% | Highest conversion |
| Applications to exceptional openings | 20% | Still useful; not the center |
| Content that reinforces expertise | 10% | One LinkedIn post / week |

Daily job scans still catch strong public roles. They are no longer the strategy center.

## Who to contact

### Companies under ~300 employees

- CEO
- Founder
- COO
- Head of Operations

### Companies above ~300 employees

- Chief Customer Officer
- VP Customer Success
- VP Operations
- VP Support
- Talent Director

**Avoid** generic recruiting inboxes on first touch.

## Messaging rules

1. **Do not ask for a job.** Start a business conversation.
2. **Give away expertise** — demonstrate value before you’re hired.
3. **Personalize** to growth stage, product, and customer model. Fewer high-quality messages beat 100 generic ones.
4. **Stay useful** on follow-ups. Do not ask whether they saw your message.

### Canonical first message

```
Hi Sarah,

I've been following your growth over the last year. As companies move from roughly 100 to 500 employees, customer operations often become the limiting factor before leadership expects it.

I spent nine years building and scaling support at Ka'Chava from a single agent to a 95+ person global organization while implementing AI, self-service, BPO operations, and Zendesk at scale.

Even if you're not hiring today, I'd enjoy exchanging notes on scaling customer operations. If nothing else, perhaps one or two ideas would be useful.

— Yahor
```

That is much stronger than “I'm looking for opportunities.”

### Value-first insight (instead of “I'd love to join”)

```
I noticed you're expanding internationally. One thing I've seen repeatedly is that contact rate spikes about 30–40% if support localization isn't planned early.
```

### Message structure checklist

- [ ] Specific hook (their growth, product, stage, or public signal)
- [ ] Problem framing (ops becomes the bottleneck 100→500)
- [ ] Proof (Ka'Chava: 1 → 95+ agents, AI / self-service / BPO at scale)
- [ ] Soft ask (exchange notes / share an idea — not “any openings?”)
- [ ] No attachment dump, no resume pitch on first touch

## Research before outreach

For each company, capture enough to write a non-generic opener:

- Growth stage and approximate headcount
- Last funding round (if known)
- Customer model (SaaS, subscription commerce, marketplace, etc.)
- Hiring signals for CX / Support / Ops / leadership
- Who the best first contact is (title + LinkedIn)
- One concrete insight you can offer

Log the contact and company in Sheets (`Companies` + `Contacts`), then send and log the touch in `Interactions`.

## Quality bar

Prefer companies where your experience solves an **imminent** business problem (rapid hiring, international expansion, AI/self-service pressure, post–Series B ops strain, subscription churn/CSAT risk). Research each one; write outreach that reflects their specific stage and model.
